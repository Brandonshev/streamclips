For StreamClips, the evidence supports a configurable **preserve the whole picture** layout for wide sources: black space above and below can carry the original title and speech captions while retaining all visual context. This is a user-preference and readability decision, **not a demonstrated way to increase views**. The crop layout remains useful when the focal subject stays central. No public example proves that black bars themselves make a video popular.

## Alpha Futures campaign gallery — 1 October 2026

The public [ContentRewards Discover gallery](https://contentrewards.com/discover) showed seven unique Alpha Futures clips ranked at approximately 360K, 146.9K, 144.9K, 132.6K, 122.6K, 120.3K and 65K views. The 360K clip opened with the specific on-screen hook “Trader Spends $66,440 on Breakfast.” Other clips placed short topic or curiosity headlines above landscape interviews or charts, kept the key scene visible, and used readable speech captions. These are observations, not proof that the editing caused the view counts; the gallery does not disclose retention, distribution, audience, or promotion. The public gallery did not expose individual post links.

StreamClips v0.1.9 therefore adds a brief opening hook selected from the clip's own transcript, retains the original source title, limits caption length, and preserves the full picture by default. The [official Alpha Futures campaign rules](https://content.clipalphafutures.com/) prohibit AI-generated clips and automated repurposing/clip tools. StreamClips output is not eligible for that campaign under the current rules; this research only informed the general visual format.

## Screenshot-guided layout — StreamClips v0.2.0

The user's two reference screenshots show a question or broader-point hook above a landscape scene and a platform-logo-plus-creator credit below it. The new renderer uses that structure with softer sentence-case type and colour emoji. The original source title moves to the prepared post caption and YouTube description. The hook is grounded in the selected transcript; a few concrete patterns can become broader questions, but the free heuristic cannot guarantee an engaging or factually flawless headline on every recording. The v0.2.0 videos are style experiments, not evidence that these choices increase reach.

In v0.2.1, the user's preferred arrangement places lowercase dialogue captions just under the footage and the lowercase creator credit below them. Hook emoji sit inline with the final line of the question. Caption phrases use a brief fade and slight scale-up. These are presentation changes requested by the user; they have not been validated against audience-retention data.

In v0.2.2, the YouTube source credit preserves the creator's usual capitalization while the hook and dialogue captions remain lowercase. This follows the user's specific preference for the creator name beside the YouTube logo.
